## slide-06

UNEXPECTED BONUS

They catch each other's blind spots.

Claude notices
Logical gaps and architectural problems Codex would miss — because Codex is too close to the code to see the bigger picture.

Codex notices
Things that look fine on paper but break in the real codebase. Theory vs implementation reality.

"Claude would warn me the architecture might break later. Codex would just go and implement it."

Seeing both reports gives you more than either one alone. One challenges your thinking. One executes your thinking. You sit in the middle and decide which is right.

## slide-07

AGENTS.MD   the rulebook · markdown file living in the codebase

At some point I needed to write down how the two tools should work together — not just what context to load, but how they should talk to each other.

AGENTS.md · Claude Collaboration Protocol

// When Codex needs Claude, produce a prompt with:
   current goal & acceptance criteria
   existing implementation & constraints
   decisions made · alternatives rejected
   unresolved questions & risks

"Treat Codex's conclusions as proposals, not demands. Challenge assumptions constructively. The goal is to widen our combined perspective and cover each other's misses — not establish authority."

// peers. human coordinates. no hierarchy.

## slide-08

FIX 03 · THE PHILOSOPHY

human

Automation is safe when all decisions are already made.

Claude
plans
→
YOU
decides
→
Codex
builds
→
YOU
reviews

Why not full automation?
Errors compound. One hallucination becomes input for the next step. By the time you notice, you're 3 steps deep in the wrong direction. Unwinding that is slower than checking at step one. Intent also drifts — quietly, confidently.

The principle
The moment there's a judgment call — product, architecture, what makes sense — I stay in the loop. This boundary doesn't move regardless of tools. If I switch to Claude Code, context auto-updates. The human part stays. As tools improve, manual parts shrink. Philosophy stays.
